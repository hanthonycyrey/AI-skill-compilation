# Common grounds for refusal or delay — structural reference

Structural context, not a database of statistics. Confirm any specific current policy detail via search when it matters to the user's case.

## Cross-cutting (all pathways)

- **PIC 4020 — false or misleading information/documents**, or non-disclosure of relevant material (e.g. an undisclosed prior visa refusal). This is often a mandatory refusal ground and can trigger a multi-year re-application bar. This is the single highest-stakes category to probe for.
- **PIC 4001/character** — undisclosed criminal history, association concerns, or (for some cases) national security concerns.
- **Health** — significant health conditions that may affect cost/community health criteria (a "public interest" style health assessment).
- **Insufficient evidence for a claim** — not fraud, just under-supported (e.g. a points claim with a thin reference letter). This causes delay/requests for more information at minimum, refusal at worst if not resolved.
- **Inconsistency across the application** — dates, job titles, or details that don't match between the EOI, the skills assessment, employment evidence, and the visa application form itself. Even innocent clerical inconsistencies invite scrutiny.
- **Identity/bona fides concerns** — where supporting documents seem inconsistent with a genuine, coherent life history.

## GSM-specific (189/190/491)

- Nominated occupation not on the correct list at both EOI and invitation time.
- Skills assessment scope not matching claimed points (e.g. claiming more skilled employment years than the assessing authority recognised).
- State nomination (190/491): application doesn't address the specific state's published criteria, or the applicant's circumstances don't match what was represented to the state.
- 491 family-sponsored stream: sponsor's own eligibility/regional residence not adequately evidenced.
- English test result expired by the time of invitation/decision.

## 482 (Skills in Demand) specific

- **Genuine position concerns** — officer unconvinced the nominated role is a genuine, ongoing operational need rather than created to bring in a particular person (more likely to be probed where there's a personal/family relationship with the sponsor, or the business is very small/new).
- **Salary below threshold or below market rate** for the role, or salary evidence that doesn't hold up (e.g. contract says one figure, other evidence suggests another).
- **Work experience not matching the nominated occupation's actual duties.**
- **Sponsor obligations/compliance history** — if the employer has sponsorship compliance issues, that's a separate risk track from the applicant's own file, but affects the overall case; worth the applicant asking their employer.
- **Labour market testing gaps** (Core Skills stream, where required) — inadequate or improperly timed advertising evidence.

## What officers weigh evidence quality on

Specificity, internal consistency, corroboration across independent sources (e.g. payslips + tax records + reference letter all agreeing), and whether the narrative around genuineness holds together — not volume of paperwork for its own sake.

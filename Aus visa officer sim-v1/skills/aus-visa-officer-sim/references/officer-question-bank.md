# Officer question style guide

Use these as inspiration for Mode A (roleplay) — adapt to the specific case rather than reading them verbatim. Ask one or two at a time, follow up based on the answer, and escalate specificity if an answer is vague.

## Employment / points claims
- "Walk me through a typical week in this role — what were you actually doing day to day?"
- "Your reference letter says [X hours/week] — does that match your payslips and super contributions for the same period?"
- "You've claimed this role as [ANZSCO occupation] — which specific duties align with that occupation's task description?"
- "There's a gap between [date] and [date] in your employment history — what were you doing then?"

## Skills assessment / points
- "Your skills assessment recognises [N] years of skilled employment — your EOI claims [M]. Can you explain the difference?"
- "Why did you choose this assessing authority / this occupation code over [an adjacent alternative]?"

## Genuineness (482 / nomination)
- "Why this employer, and why this role, specifically?"
- "What does this position add to the business that couldn't be filled locally?" (Core Skills stream)
- "What's your relationship, if any, to anyone at the sponsoring business?"
- "What happens to this role if your visa isn't approved — does the business still need it filled?"

## State nomination (190/491)
- "Why this state specifically, and how does your occupation match what they've said they need?"
- "What ties do you have to this state or region beyond the nomination itself?"

## Consistency / character
- "I'm seeing [detail] in one document and [different detail] in another — which is correct, and why the discrepancy?"
- "Have you had any prior visa refusals, cancellations, or overstays in any country? Tell me about all of them, not just the ones you think matter."

## Closing
- "Is there anything about your application you're unsure is strong enough, that you'd rather raise now than have questioned later?"

After roleplay, always end by summarising: what held up well under questioning, what needs more evidence, and what needs a genuinely different answer (not just better phrasing).

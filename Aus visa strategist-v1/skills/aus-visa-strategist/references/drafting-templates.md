# Drafting guidance

All drafts must be built from facts the user actually provides. If input is thin, ask follow-up questions for concrete specifics (dates, project names, numbers, tools used, team size) rather than filling gaps with plausible-sounding generic language — generic statements read as weaker (and to a trained officer, as a red flag) regardless of who wrote them.

## Statement addressing a specific claim (e.g. employment duties, points claim)

Structure:
1. What is being claimed and why it matters to the application (one line)
2. Chronological, specific account: dates, employer, role, concrete duties/responsibilities matched to the relevant ANZSCO description language where genuinely accurate
3. Evidence the statement is backed by (reference this — "as shown in the attached reference letter and payslips")
4. Keep it factual, not promotional — avoid superlatives with nothing behind them

## Employer reference letter checklist (to hand to the employer, not to draft on their behalf)

The user should ask their employer/HR to include:
- Company letterhead, contact details, ABN if available
- Applicant's exact job title and employment dates (start, and end or "current")
- Full-time/part-time status and hours per week
- Detailed list of actual duties performed (specific enough to match ANZSCO task descriptions)
- Salary/remuneration
- Signed by someone with authority to confirm employment (manager/HR, with their title)

Never draft this letter to submit as if the employer wrote it — that's misrepresentation. You can draft the *checklist* the user gives their employer, or a *template* the employer can adapt in their own words.

## Genuine position / genuine temporary entrant style narratives

Structure:
1. Applicant's background and how it led here (specific, not generic)
2. For 482: why this specific role, why this employer, how the role fits the business's genuine operational need
3. Ties and circumstances relevant to the genuineness assessment
4. Future intentions, framed honestly

## Cover letter for skills assessment / nomination submission

Structure: short intro (occupation, purpose), bullet list of enclosed documents mapped to the assessing body's stated requirements, any clarifying notes (e.g. name discrepancies across documents, explained), contact details.

## Style notes

- Plain, specific, factual language over flowery language.
- Every substantive sentence should be checkable against an attached document.
- Keep statements consistent with dates/details elsewhere in the application — a mismatch is a self-inflicted red flag (this is exactly what the companion "officer" skill will stress-test).

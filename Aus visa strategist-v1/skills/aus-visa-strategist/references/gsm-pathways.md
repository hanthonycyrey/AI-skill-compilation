# General Skilled Migration (GSM): 189 / 190 / 491 — structural reference

This is structural/methodology context, not current numbers. Confirm any specific figure (points, thresholds, occupation list membership) via web search against immi.homeaffairs.gov.au or SkillSelect before relying on it.

## The three pathways at a glance

- **189 (Skilled Independent)** — permanent, no state or family sponsorship needed, invited on points alone via SkillSelect. Most competitive; typically needs the highest points score of the three.
- **190 (Skilled Nominated)** — permanent, requires state/territory government nomination (adds points), applicant usually commits to specific state criteria/occupation demand.
- **491 (Skilled Work Regional, provisional)** — 5-year visa, requires state/territory nomination or eligible family sponsorship in a designated regional area (adds more points than 190), leads to permanent residence via subclass 191 after a qualifying period living/working regionally.

All three share the same core eligibility spine:

## Core eligibility spine (all three)

1. **Nominated occupation** on the relevant skilled occupation list at both EOI and invitation time, correctly matched to an ANZSCO code.
2. **Positive skills assessment** from the relevant assessing authority for that occupation — this is often the single longest lead-time item (weeks to months).
3. **Points test score** meeting at least the minimum to be invited (points come from: age, English proficiency, skilled employment — onshore/offshore, qualifications, partner skills, state/regional nomination, Australian study, community language, professional year, etc.). Minimum-to-invite is not the same as competitive — invited scores are often meaningfully higher and vary by occupation and round.
4. **Competent (or higher) English** via an approved test (IELTS, PTE, TOEFL, Cambridge, OET) — results have a validity window, check it's still current at invitation.
5. **Age limit** at time of invitation.
6. **Health and character** — medicals, police clearances from every country lived in 12+ months over the relevant look-back period.
7. **Expression of Interest (EOI)** via SkillSelect, then (for 190/491) nomination application to the state/territory, then invitation, then the visa application itself with full documentary evidence for every claim made in the EOI.

## Where applications actually get weak (evidence-wise)

- Employment evidence for claimed points-relevant work: needs to show duties matching the ANZSCO description, hours (full-time equivalent), dates, and remuneration — payslips + super statements + tax records + a detailed reference letter, not just a letter alone.
- Gaps or overlaps in employment history that aren't explained.
- Skills assessment scope not matching the actual EOI claims (e.g. assessed years of experience differing from EOI-claimed years).
- State nomination (190/491): not addressing the specific state's published criteria/occupation demand — generic applications are weaker than ones that speak to the state's actual priorities.
- 491 family-sponsored stream: sponsor's eligibility and regional residence itself needs to be evidenced, not just the relationship.

## Processing priority note

Ministerial Directions periodically reset processing priority order across skilled subclasses (which nominations/visas get looked at first). This changes and affects realistic timing expectations more than eligibility — mention it when discussing timelines, and search for the current direction if the user asks about wait times.

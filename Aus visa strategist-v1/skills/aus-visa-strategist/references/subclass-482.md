# Subclass 482 — Skills in Demand (SID) visa — structural reference

Replaced the old Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) visa in Dec 2024, keeping the "482" subclass number. Structural context only — confirm current thresholds, streams, and the occupation list via web search before relying on specific figures.

## Three streams

- **Core Skills** — occupation must be on the Core Skills Occupation List (CSOL); salary must meet the Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT) or market rate, whichever is higher.
- **Specialist Skills** — no occupation list restriction, but salary must clear the (materially higher) Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT).
- **Labour Agreement** — occupation and terms set by the employer's specific labour agreement with the Department.

CSIT/SSIT (and the related TSMIT used for 186/187/494) are indexed roughly annually, typically each 1 July — always confirm the current number rather than quoting a remembered one.

## Three linked applications, all must succeed

1. **Standard Business Sponsorship (employer)** — employer must be lawfully operating, meet training benchmark obligations, and commit to sponsor obligations.
2. **Nomination (employer)** — specific role nominated, occupation matched to CSOL/ANZSCO (Core Skills) or shown genuinely specialist (Specialist Skills), salary at/above threshold and at/above market rate for an equivalent Australian worker, Skilling Australians Fund (SAF) levy paid. Core Skills stream typically requires labour market testing (evidence the role was genuinely advertised to Australians first) unless an exemption applies.
3. **Visa application (applicant)** — occupation match, generally ~1+ years relevant work experience in the nominated occupation or closely related field (measured over a defined recent window), English proficiency (often IELTS ~5.0 overall/4.5 per band or equivalent, some exemptions apply), health and character.

## Where 482 applications get weak (evidence-wise)

- **Genuine position**: the case officer can query whether the nominated position is a genuine, ongoing need of the business, not manufactured to bring in a specific person. Evidence: org chart, business operations detail, why this role/skill level is needed now.
- **Work experience matching**: experience evidence must line up with the nominated occupation's actual duties, not just a similar-sounding job title.
- **Salary evidence**: contract, market salary rate justification, and how it compares to what Australian employees in equivalent roles are paid — under-threshold or unsubstantiated salary claims are a common issue.
- **Employer sponsor compliance history**: if the sponsoring employer has prior sponsorship compliance issues, that affects the case independent of the applicant's own evidence — worth the applicant asking their employer about this directly.
- **Relationship between applicant and employer**: if there's a personal/family relationship with the sponsor, expect closer scrutiny of genuineness.

## PR pathway note

Many 482 holders become eligible for permanent residence via subclass 186 (Employer Nomination Scheme), often after a qualifying period with an approved sponsor — mention this as a strategic consideration but confirm current qualifying periods via search, they have changed in recent years.

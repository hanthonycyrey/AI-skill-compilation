# Assessing authority frameworks — structural reference

Structural context only. Confirm current authority-to-occupation mapping, fees, and processing times via web search — these are published by each authority and updated periodically.

## Common assessing authorities and their pathway style

- **ACS (Australian Computer Society)** — most ICT occupations. Uses an RPL (Recognised Prior Learning) style report when the applicant's qualification isn't closely aligned to ICT (an "ICT major" vs "non-major" distinction affects how much skilled employment is required/counted). Requires a project report / employment reference structure mapping duties to the ANZSCO occupation.
- **Engineers Australia (EA)** — most engineering occupations (263xxx/233xxx range broadly, but confirm the exact code — some ICT-adjacent codes like network/systems engineering sit with EA, most software roles sit with ACS). Uses the Competency Demonstration Report (CDR): three Career Episodes (each ~1,000–2,500 words, first person, numbered paragraphs), one Summary Statement (cross-referencing competency elements to specific paragraphs), a CPD (Continuing Professional Development) log, and a CV. EA uses plagiarism and AI-content detection — originality and personal specificity are essential, not optional polish.
- **VETASSESS** — a wide range of professional and trade/other occupations not covered by a specific body. Typically assesses qualifications plus employment (a minimum period of relevant skilled employment, assessed against the ANZSCO description), often via a detailed employment reference form plus supporting evidence.
- **CPA Australia / Chartered Accountants ANZ (CA ANZ) / IPA** — accounting occupations, each with their own membership/assessment pathway and units-of-competency requirements if the qualification doesn't fully align with the Australian accounting body of knowledge.
- **ANMAC (Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council)** — nursing/midwifery, generally involves qualification comparison and sometimes bridging/registration steps beyond the migration assessment itself.
- **TRA (Trades Recognition Australia)** — trade occupations, multiple possible pathways (e.g. offshore skills assessment, Job Ready Program for onshore applicants) depending on where the applicant is and their qualification/experience mix.

## What's usually different between assessment evidence and visa evidence

- Assessment often wants a *narrative/technical* account of what the applicant actually did (CDR career episodes, RPL project reports), not just a generic HR reference letter.
- Assessment employment requirements (what counts as "closely related," how many years) can differ from how points are calculated on the visa side — a role can be accepted for assessment purposes but not fully align with EOI points claims, or vice versa. Always sanity-check assessment scope against what's being claimed in the EOI.
- Recency windows matter and vary by authority (e.g. how far back qualifying work experience can be drawn from) — confirm current windows rather than assuming.

## Cross-checking with the points claim

Once an assessment outcome is in hand, always compare what years/roles it recognised against what the applicant intends to claim points for in the EOI — a mismatch here is one of the most common self-inflicted weaknesses (see the companion officer-simulator skill's refusal-reasons reference).
